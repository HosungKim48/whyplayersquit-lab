Recommended

94% of recommenders keep playing

83% played 10 or more further hours, and 12% were active in the two weeks before the snapshot. Median play after a positive review: 54.7 hours.

Not recommended

Critics stop about 5 times as often, at every stage of play

32% of critics logged under 1 more hour, against 6% of recommenders. Hours played does not explain the gap: recommenders with the critics' mix of hours would stop 7% of the time. Still, 68% of critics played on and 51% played 10+ more hours.

Recommended, then gone

18,390 reviewers recommended a game and stopped there

A small group that a review score never shows. It is largest in Dragon's Dogma 2: 19% of recommenders who reviewed at 50–200 h logged under 1 more hour, against 9% at that stage across all six games. That pattern fits finishing a game more than quitting it.

How far this goes. Reviews represent only the players who wrote them. All figures are shares of reviewers, and every pattern is an association, not a cause. Stopping is not the same as a lost customer: these are paid games, and a reviewer who stops after 80 hours may simply have finished, while one who stops after 2 never got started. Read each walk-away rate next to hours played at review. See Method & limits.

Why players quit

Critics who stop are not spread evenly across complaints. Players who report crashes are the ones who stay. Reviewers left clearly less often than other critics after complaints about crashes & bugs (6 of 6 games), online, accounts & drm (4 of 6 games). They left clearly more often after complaints about gameplay & design choices (5 of 6 games), price & monetization (4 of 6 games). After controls, every other complaint type has higher odds of walking away than crash complaints (1.19×–3.15×). See which complaints go with stopping.

How it works

Steam publishes, next to every review, how long the reviewer had played when they wrote it and how long they have played in total. The gap shows what the reviewer did after reviewing. For negative reviews, a language model also sorts each one into one main complaint, in its original language, which separates complaints that travel with quitting from complaints players voice while they stay.

Why these six: premium games with a large body of negative reviews (36%–53% negative) and a review history small enough to fetch in full without straining Steam, across different genres. In a second-model check of 170 random labels across languages, 92% of main reasons were judged correct. Total paid model spend for the project: $14.96. The code can be re-run on any Steam app ID.

Prioritize

Not enough content is the complaint that goes with leaving

12% of negative reviews; 28% of these reviewers logged under 1 more hour, against 24% for all negative reviewers. Also above average: gameplay & design choices, post-launch support. The loudest group that mostly stays: performance (28% of reviews, 21% walked away).

Protect

The first 2 hours lose 41% of early critics

77% of reviews written with under 2 hours played are negative (5,413 reviews), and 41% of those reviewers never logged another hour. Median playtime at a negative review: 60.7 h.

Win back

9% of critics said they would come back

8,395 reviewers wrote that they are waiting for fixes; 64% of them went on to play 10+ more hours. Overall, 60% of negative reviewers played 10+ more hours and 6% played in the two weeks before the snapshot.

How far this goes. Reviews come only from players who chose to write one, so every share is a share of reviewers, not of all players. "Walked away" is read from public playtime (22,809 of 93,718 negative reviews at least 30 days old). Everything below is an association to check against your own telemetry, not proof of cause.

7 · Checks by team

Reviews show where to look. Each lead below can be confirmed or rejected with internal telemetry.

EngineeringPerformance28% of negative reviews▼ Stay more often

21% of these reviewers walked away; median 31.0 hours played at review.
  • Compare session length and next-day return by hardware tier in your own telemetry; reviews cannot see hardware.
  • Publish a performance target per spec tier and report against it in patch notes, so waiting players know when to return.

Engineering & QACrashes & bugs16% of negative reviews▼ Stay more often

16% of these reviewers walked away; median 32.0 hours played at review.
  • Rank crash signatures and save-loss bugs by how many players hit them in their first sessions, not by ticket count.
  • Check whether players who crashed in session one or two ever started a third session.

ProductionNot enough content12% of negative reviews▲ Leave more often

28% of these reviewers walked away; median 86.8 hours played at review.
  • Find the hour mark where these reviews cluster and look at what players have run out of by then.
  • State on the store page what is in the game today versus planned, so expectations match the build.

Game designGameplay & design choices11% of negative reviews▲ Leave more often

26% of these reviewers walked away; median 93.8 hours played at review.
  • Separate 'I dislike this mechanic' from 'this mechanic is unclear': the first is a design call, the second is onboarding.
  • Test optional rule settings or modes for the most-named mechanic and measure retention of players who switch them on.

Live ops & communityPost-launch support5% of negative reviews▲ Leave more often

29% of these reviewers walked away; median 99.3 hours played at review.
  • Audit promised-versus-shipped items in public posts; these reviewers are usually long-time players reacting to a specific change.
  • After a patch that triggers a review spike, compare return rates of players active the week before with earlier weeks.

8 · Limits & data

  • Only people who wrote a review; they are a small and unusual part of the player base.
  • Playtime after a review is Steam's public figure at the snapshot (2026-11-24). Offline play and other platforms are not counted. Reviews newer than 30 days are left out of walk-away rates.
  • Editing a review may refresh its "playtime at review" figure. Without edited reviews the walk-away rate of negative reviewers is 24% (with them: 24%).
  • Reason labels come from a language model reading up to 1,200 characters per review in its original language. In a second-model check of 170 random labels across languages, 92% of main reasons were judged correct and 93% of quit-statement tags; expect roughly that error rate in reason shares.
  • "Walked away" is set at under 1 more hour. At under 2 hours the negative-reviewer rate is 27% and 10 of 10 complaint groups keep the same verdict; at under 5 hours it is 33% and 10 of 10 keep it (rank correlation with the 1-hour ordering: 1.00 and 0.99).
  • Groups that complain about different things also differ in hours played, purchase timing and expectations; the regression in section 1 controls for some of this, not all.

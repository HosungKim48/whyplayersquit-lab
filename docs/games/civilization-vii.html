Prioritize

Price & monetization critics leave most often

40% of these reviewers logged under 1 more hour, against 36% for all negative reviewers (4 points higher), on 8% of negative reviews. The largest group above average is gameplay & design choices (33% of reviews), where the gap is 1 point (37%). The loudest group that mostly stays: not enough content (15% of reviews, 31% walked away).

Protect

65% of under-2-hour critics stop there, inside Steam's refund window

87% of reviews written with under 2 hours played are negative (3,878 reviews), and 65% of those reviewers never logged another hour. Steam refunds are available up to 2 hours of play, so some of them stopped to stay eligible, whatever they complained about (5% of all negative reviews carry the refund flag). Median playtime at a negative review: 14.7 h.

Win back

12% of critics said they would come back

2,535 reviewers wrote that they are waiting for fixes; 54% of them went on to play 10+ more hours. Overall, 46% of negative reviewers played 10+ more hours and 9% played in the two weeks before the snapshot.

How far this goes. Reviews come only from players who chose to write one, so every share is a share of reviewers, not of all players. "Walked away" is read from public playtime (8,001 of 22,405 negative reviews at least 30 days old). Stopping is not the same as a lost customer: this is a paid game and the median critic had already played 14.7 hours, so a reviewer who stops late may simply have finished. Read each walk-away rate next to hours played at review. Everything below is an association to check against your own telemetry, not proof of cause.

Does it hold after controls?

A logistic regression on 21,424 of this game's labeled negative reviews predicts walking away from the main complaint while holding constant hours played at review, review language, how long after launch the review was written, and whether it was edited. Each row compares a complaint with crashes & bugs: 2.00× means twice the odds of having walked away. This narrows the comparison; it still does not show cause. Reviewers whose main complaint was crashes & bugs are the comparison group because they stay most often, so each row reads as how much more often that group walked away than they did. Reviews with no clear reason sit at 2.58×, higher than 7 of the 8 named complaint types, so the complaint label explains only part of who leaves.

6 · Player voice (paraphrased)

Short paraphrases that show the kind of complaint in each group. They are not quotes, and no review text is published.

Gameplay & design choices

Removing age transitions entirely, or adding a classic mode, would make the reviewer switch to a positive rating.
The game feels like a major regression from earlier Civ entries, and the execution seems poor compared to Humankind.

Not enough content

A major step backward: an unfinished product whose three-stage age system punishes players who play well.
Needs many updates across the board and feels like an early access release rather than a finished game.

Interface & controls

A scam, with uncontrollable behavior, English input defaulting, and map scrolling during hands-off play; the reviewer calls it garbage.

Crashes & bugs

Not polished; disappointing given the studio's track record, with auto-generated maps, weak diplomacy, poor UI info, and messy age transitions.
Very poorly optimized: repeated crashes on a high-end PC made the game unplayable, so the reviewer got a refund.

7 · Checks by team

Reviews show where to look. Each lead below can be confirmed or rejected with internal telemetry.

Game designGameplay & design choices33% of negative reviews▲ Leave more often

37% of these reviewers walked away; median 19.7 hours played at review.
  • Separate 'I dislike this mechanic' from 'this mechanic is unclear': the first is a design call, the second is onboarding.
  • Test optional rule settings or modes for the most-named mechanic and measure retention of players who switch them on.

ProductionNot enough content15% of negative reviews▼ Stay more often

31% of these reviewers walked away; median 16.1 hours played at review.
  • Find the hour mark where these reviews cluster and look at what players have run out of by then.
  • State on the store page what is in the game today versus planned, so expectations match the build.

UX / UIInterface & controls14% of negative reviews● About average

35% of these reviewers walked away; median 7.1 hours played at review.
  • Run task-based usability checks on the screens named most often; interface complaints are specific and cheap to verify.
  • Ship interface fixes in small, frequent patches and list them first in the notes.

Engineering & QACrashes & bugs12% of negative reviews▼ Stay more often

24% of these reviewers walked away; median 10.9 hours played at review.
  • Rank crash signatures and save-loss bugs by how many players hit them in their first sessions, not by ticket count.
  • Check whether players who crashed in session one or two ever started a third session.

Publishing & pricingPrice & monetization8% of negative reviews▲ Leave more often

40% of these reviewers walked away; median 23.1 hours played at review.
  • Compare refund and early-exit rates across editions, regions and price points; price complaints concentrate before much play.
  • Check whether paid add-ons land while core complaints are still open; the spike list shows review timing around such posts.

8 · Limits & data

  • Only people who wrote a review; they are a small and unusual part of the player base.
  • Playtime after a review is Steam's public figure at the snapshot (2026-11-24). Offline play and other platforms are not counted. Reviews newer than 30 days are left out of walk-away rates.
  • Editing a review may refresh its "playtime at review" figure. Without edited reviews the walk-away rate of negative reviewers is 36% (with them: 36%).
  • Reason labels come from a language model reading up to 1,200 characters per review in its original language. In a second-model check of 170 random labels across languages, 92% of main reasons were judged correct and 93% of quit-statement tags; expect roughly that error rate in reason shares.
  • "Walked away" is set at under 1 more hour. At under 2 hours the negative-reviewer rate is 39% and 10 of 10 complaint groups keep the same verdict; at under 5 hours it is 47% and 10 of 10 keep it (rank correlation with the 1-hour ordering: 0.96 and 1.00).
  • Groups that complain about different things also differ in hours played, purchase timing and expectations; the regression in section 1 controls for some of this, not all.

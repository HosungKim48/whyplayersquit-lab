Quit-signal report · 4X strategy · released 2025-02-11 · data snapshot 2026-11-24

Sid Meier's Civilization VII

Who left after reviewing, when they gave up, and which complaints go with actually leaving — from every public English Steam review.

Bottom line
  • 49% of the 29,077 English reviews are negative. The median negative review was written after 15.1 hours of play (positive: 25.2 hours).
  • A negative review is not the same as quitting. 38% of negative reviewers logged less than 1 more hour after posting; the other 62% kept playing, and 44% played 10 or more further hours. Among positive reviewers, 4% stopped that quickly.
  • The most common main complaints are gameplay & design choices (36%), not enough content (15%), interface & controls (14%), out of 13,712 labeled negative reviews.
  • Compared with all negative reviewers (38%), reviewers walked away clearly more often after complaints about gameplay & design choices (39%); and clearly less often after complaints about crashes & bugs (25%), not enough content (33%). These are associations, not causes.
  • The first two hours are the sharpest edge: 88% of reviews written with under 2 hours played are negative, and 68% of those reviewers did not come back for another hour.
  • What players say and do differ: of reviewers who wrote that they had quit, 49% still logged another hour or more. Of those who wrote they would wait for fixes, 52% went on to play 10+ more hours.

5. In players' words (paraphrased)

These are short paraphrases written to show the kind of complaint in each group. They are not quotes, and no review text is published.

Gameplay & design choices

A player says the game feels shallow and rushed, with good ideas buried under weak design and console-style simplification.
A player quit after three weeks, saying the game is dull and nothing like the fun of the prior version.

Not enough content

A player finds the game unpolished, lacking charm, and got bored after about eleven hours.
A player calls the game incomplete, with poor interface design, repetitive maps, and an underdeveloped core loop.

Interface & controls

A player says the game is hard to understand, obscures how features work, and regrets buying it.
A player says the UI is messy and finds it odd that some chosen historical figures never led their civilization.

Crashes & bugs

A player says the game won't launch despite meeting the requirements that run other demanding titles without trouble.
A player says the game fails in DirectX 12 mode while the previous version works, calling the game bad.

Price & monetization

A player says it's early-access quality at full price, with unclear UI, hard-to-read tiles, samey AI, and unfamiliar leaders.

6. What a studio could do with this

Reviews point at where to look; a studio's own telemetry can confirm or reject each lead. Suggested checks for this game's largest complaint groups:

Gameplay & design choices36% of labeled negative reviews
39% of these reviewers walked away (higher vs. all negative reviewers); median 19.6 hours played at review.
  • Separate 'I don't like this mechanic' from 'this mechanic is unclear': the first is a design choice, the second is onboarding.
  • Test optional rule settings or modes for the most-named mechanic, and measure retention of players who switch them on.
Not enough content15% of labeled negative reviews
33% of these reviewers walked away (lower vs. all negative reviewers); median 16.6 hours played at review.
  • Find the hour mark where these reviews cluster and look at what players have run out of by then.
  • Say plainly on the store page what is in the game today versus planned, so expectations match the build.
Interface & controls14% of labeled negative reviews
37% of these reviewers walked away (similar vs. all negative reviewers); median 7.3 hours played at review.
  • Run task-based usability checks on the screens named most often; UI complaints tend to be specific and cheap to verify.
  • Ship interface fixes in small, frequent patches and list them at the top of the notes; these reviewers often keep playing.
Crashes & bugs11% of labeled negative reviews
25% of these reviewers walked away (lower vs. all negative reviewers); median 11.9 hours played at review.
  • Rank crash signatures and save-loss bugs by how many players hit them in their first sessions, not by ticket count.
  • Check whether players who crashed in session one or two ever started a third session.
Three uses of this report
  • Prioritize by behaviour, not volume. Put a complaint group higher when its authors walk away more often than other negative reviewers (section 2, right-hand chart).
  • Protect the riskiest hours. Section 1 shows where negative reviews and walk-aways concentrate; that is the stretch of the game to instrument and playtest first.
  • Talk to the "waiting" group. Section 3 sizes the reviewers who said they would return after fixes and shows how many did; patch notes aimed at their stated reasons are the natural test.

Limits of this report

  • Only English reviews. Only people who wrote a review; they are a small and unusual part of the player base.
  • Playtime after a review is Steam's public figure at the snapshot date (2026-11-24). Offline play and play on other platforms are not counted. Reviews newer than 30 days are excluded from walk-away rates.
  • If a reviewer edited their review, Steam may have refreshed the "playtime at review" figure. Without edited reviews the walk-away rate of negative reviewers is 38% (with them: 38%).
  • Reason labels come from a language model reading up to 1,200 characters per review, one main reason each. In a hand check of 60 random labels, 88% of main reasons were judged correct and 95% of quit-statement tags; expect roughly that error rate in reason shares.
  • Associations only. Players who complain about different things also differ in other ways (hours played, when they bought, what they expected).

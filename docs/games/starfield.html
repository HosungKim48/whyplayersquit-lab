Quit-signal report · Single-player RPG · released 2023-09-06 · data snapshot 2026-11-24

Starfield

Who left after reviewing, when they gave up, and which complaints go with actually leaving — from every public English Steam review.

Bottom line
  • 42% of the 85,455 English reviews are negative. The median negative review was written after 46.5 hours of play (positive: 50.2 hours).
  • A negative review is not the same as quitting. 51% of negative reviewers logged less than 1 more hour after posting; the other 49% kept playing, and 35% played 10 or more further hours. Among positive reviewers, 7% stopped that quickly.
  • The most common main complaints are gameplay & design choices (23%), not enough content (23%), crashes & bugs (10%), out of 33,672 labeled negative reviews.
  • Compared with all negative reviewers (51%), reviewers walked away clearly more often after complaints about gameplay & design choices (55%), story & writing (54%); and clearly less often after complaints about crashes & bugs (30%), interface & controls (42%), post-launch support (43%). These are associations, not causes.
  • The first two hours are the sharpest edge: 79% of reviews written with under 2 hours played are negative, and 72% of those reviewers did not come back for another hour.
  • What players say and do differ: of reviewers who wrote that they had quit, 42% still logged another hour or more. Of those who wrote they would wait for fixes, 45% went on to play 10+ more hours.

Not enough content

A player says the game feels unfinished and advises waiting a year before buying, given Bethesda's release history.

6. What a studio could do with this

Reviews point at where to look; a studio's own telemetry can confirm or reject each lead. Suggested checks for this game's largest complaint groups:

Gameplay & design choices23% of labeled negative reviews
55% of these reviewers walked away (higher vs. all negative reviewers); median 41.6 hours played at review.
  • Separate 'I don't like this mechanic' from 'this mechanic is unclear': the first is a design choice, the second is onboarding.
  • Test optional rule settings or modes for the most-named mechanic, and measure retention of players who switch them on.
Not enough content23% of labeled negative reviews
50% of these reviewers walked away (similar vs. all negative reviewers); median 62.5 hours played at review.
  • Find the hour mark where these reviews cluster and look at what players have run out of by then.
  • Say plainly on the store page what is in the game today versus planned, so expectations match the build.
Crashes & bugs10% of labeled negative reviews
30% of these reviewers walked away (lower vs. all negative reviewers); median 53.0 hours played at review.
  • Rank crash signatures and save-loss bugs by how many players hit them in their first sessions, not by ticket count.
  • Check whether players who crashed in session one or two ever started a third session.
Performance10% of labeled negative reviews
48% of these reviewers walked away (lower vs. all negative reviewers); median 16.1 hours played at review.
  • Compare session length and return rate by hardware tier in your own telemetry; reviews cannot see hardware.
  • Publish the performance target per spec tier and report progress against it in patch notes, so waiting players know when to come back.
Three uses of this report
  • Prioritize by behaviour, not volume. Put a complaint group higher when its authors walk away more often than other negative reviewers (section 2, right-hand chart).
  • Protect the riskiest hours. Section 1 shows where negative reviews and walk-aways concentrate; that is the stretch of the game to instrument and playtest first.
  • Talk to the "waiting" group. Section 3 sizes the reviewers who said they would return after fixes and shows how many did; patch notes aimed at their stated reasons are the natural test.

Limits of this report

  • Only English reviews. Only people who wrote a review; they are a small and unusual part of the player base.
  • Playtime after a review is Steam's public figure at the snapshot date (2026-11-24). Offline play and play on other platforms are not counted. Reviews newer than 30 days are excluded from walk-away rates.
  • If a reviewer edited their review, Steam may have refreshed the "playtime at review" figure. Without edited reviews the walk-away rate of negative reviewers is 56% (with them: 51%).
  • Reason labels come from a language model reading up to 1,200 characters per review, one main reason each. In a hand check of 60 random labels, 88% of main reasons were judged correct and 95% of quit-statement tags; expect roughly that error rate in reason shares.
  • Associations only. Players who complain about different things also differ in other ways (hours played, when they bought, what they expected).

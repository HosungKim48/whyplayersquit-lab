Quit-signal report · City builder · released 2023-10-24 · data snapshot 2026-11-24

Cities: Skylines II

Who left after reviewing, when they gave up, and which complaints go with actually leaving — from every public English Steam review.

Bottom line
  • 43% of the 34,666 English reviews are negative. The median negative review was written after 22.7 hours of play (positive: 23.6 hours).
  • A negative review is not the same as quitting. 26% of negative reviewers logged less than 1 more hour after posting; the other 74% kept playing, and 59% played 10 or more further hours. Among positive reviewers, 4% stopped that quickly.
  • The most common main complaints are crashes & bugs (29%), performance (26%), not enough content (13%), out of 14,085 labeled negative reviews.
  • Compared with all negative reviewers (26%), reviewers walked away clearly more often after complaints about interface & controls (34%), performance (33%), price & monetization (30%); and clearly less often after complaints about crashes & bugs (17%), not enough content (21%), post-launch support (22%). These are associations, not causes.
  • The first two hours are the sharpest edge: 70% of reviews written with under 2 hours played are negative, and 50% of those reviewers did not come back for another hour.
  • What players say and do differ: of reviewers who wrote that they had quit, 65% still logged another hour or more. Of those who wrote they would wait for fixes, 63% went on to play 10+ more hours.

5. In players' words (paraphrased)

These are short paraphrases written to show the kind of complaint in each group. They are not quotes, and no review text is published.

Crashes & bugs

A $70 price with autosaves off led to lost hours after a GPU crash, and many achievements are broken.
The player wants to play smoothly, but random crashes make that impossible and they hope fixes come.

Performance

Good potential, but updates are slow, and the game needs many bug fixes and optimization.
The screen turns yellow when placing objects, making the game unplayable for this player.

Not enough content

Feels too similar to the original, with low frame rates, few tools, no mod support, and few props and buildings.
The player refunded it, finding it unfinished, laggy, missing features, including mods, with no developer communication.

Gameplay & design choices

Compared to the first game, it feels unbalanced, with odd speed differences that left the player disappointed.
Not recommended: the first game earns money far better, and this one leaves cities in debt even with resources.

Post-launch support

Mods are delayed in favor of console work, despite promises, and the Steam Workshop was removed for the console version.
Two years on, crashes persist, the lack of Steam Workshop hurts, and the developers ignore feedback while making assets.

6. What a studio could do with this

Reviews point at where to look; a studio's own telemetry can confirm or reject each lead. Suggested checks for this game's largest complaint groups:

Crashes & bugs29% of labeled negative reviews
17% of these reviewers walked away (lower vs. all negative reviewers); median 39.0 hours played at review.
  • Rank crash signatures and save-loss bugs by how many players hit them in their first sessions, not by ticket count.
  • Check whether players who crashed in session one or two ever started a third session.
Performance26% of labeled negative reviews
33% of these reviewers walked away (higher vs. all negative reviewers); median 3.6 hours played at review.
  • Compare session length and return rate by hardware tier in your own telemetry; reviews cannot see hardware.
  • Publish the performance target per spec tier and report progress against it in patch notes, so waiting players know when to come back.
Not enough content13% of labeled negative reviews
21% of these reviewers walked away (lower vs. all negative reviewers); median 27.4 hours played at review.
  • Find the hour mark where these reviews cluster and look at what players have run out of by then.
  • Say plainly on the store page what is in the game today versus planned, so expectations match the build.
Gameplay & design choices9% of labeled negative reviews
24% of these reviewers walked away (similar vs. all negative reviewers); median 26.5 hours played at review.
  • Separate 'I don't like this mechanic' from 'this mechanic is unclear': the first is a design choice, the second is onboarding.
  • Test optional rule settings or modes for the most-named mechanic, and measure retention of players who switch them on.
Three uses of this report
  • Prioritize by behaviour, not volume. Put a complaint group higher when its authors walk away more often than other negative reviewers (section 2, right-hand chart).
  • Protect the riskiest hours. Section 1 shows where negative reviews and walk-aways concentrate; that is the stretch of the game to instrument and playtest first.
  • Talk to the "waiting" group. Section 3 sizes the reviewers who said they would return after fixes and shows how many did; patch notes aimed at their stated reasons are the natural test.

Limits of this report

  • Only English reviews. Only people who wrote a review; they are a small and unusual part of the player base.
  • Playtime after a review is Steam's public figure at the snapshot date (2026-11-24). Offline play and play on other platforms are not counted. Reviews newer than 30 days are excluded from walk-away rates.
  • If a reviewer edited their review, Steam may have refreshed the "playtime at review" figure. Without edited reviews the walk-away rate of negative reviewers is 28% (with them: 26%).
  • Reason labels come from a language model reading up to 1,200 characters per review, one main reason each. In a hand check of 60 random labels, 88% of main reasons were judged correct and 95% of quit-statement tags; expect roughly that error rate in reason shares.
  • Associations only. Players who complain about different things also differ in other ways (hours played, when they bought, what they expected).

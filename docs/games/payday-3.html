Prioritize

Price & monetization critics leave most often

41% stopped, against 32% of all critics. 7% of negative reviews. Largest group above average: not enough content (12% of reviews, 39% stopped). Loud but staying: online, accounts & drm (37% of reviews, 22% stopped).

Protect

47% of under-2-hour critics stop there, inside Steam's refund window

81% of under-2-hour reviews are negative. Steam refunds are available up to 2 hours of play.

Win back

10% of critics said they'd come back

2,541 said they were waiting for fixes; 51% of them played 10+ more hours.

A few cautions: these are reviewers, not all players, so check the patterns against your own telemetry. The typical critic had already played 7.9 hours, so stopping late can just mean finishing. Method & limits

7 · Checks by team

Show the checks for each team

Reviews show where to look. Each lead below can be confirmed or rejected with internal telemetry.

Online servicesOnline, accounts & DRM37% of negative reviews▼ Stay more often

22% of these reviewers walked away; median 4.9 hours played at review.
  • Track failed logins, disconnects and matchmaking time-outs per player and tie them to next-day return.
  • If an account or always-online requirement is named, measure how many players drop at that exact step.

ProductionNot enough content12% of negative reviews▲ Leave more often

39% of these reviewers walked away; median 12.3 hours played at review.
  • Find the hour mark where these reviews cluster and look at what players have run out of by then.
  • State on the store page what is in the game today versus planned, so expectations match the build.

Game designGameplay & design choices10% of negative reviews▲ Leave more often

36% of these reviewers walked away; median 13.5 hours played at review.
  • Separate 'I dislike this mechanic' from 'this mechanic is unclear': the first is a design call, the second is onboarding.
  • Test optional rule settings or modes for the most-named mechanic and measure retention of players who switch them on.

Engineering & QACrashes & bugs8% of negative reviews▼ Stay more often

27% of these reviewers walked away; median 6.0 hours played at review.
  • Rank crash signatures and save-loss bugs by how many players hit them in their first sessions, not by ticket count.
  • Check whether players who crashed in session one or two ever started a third session.

Publishing & pricingPrice & monetization7% of negative reviews▲ Leave more often

41% of these reviewers walked away; median 11.9 hours played at review.
  • Compare refund and early-exit rates across editions, regions and price points; price complaints concentrate before much play.
  • Check whether paid add-ons land while core complaints are still open; the spike list shows review timing around such posts.

8 · Limits & data

Show limits and data files
  • Only people who wrote a review; they are a small and unusual part of the player base.
  • Playtime after a review is Steam's public figure at the snapshot (2026-11-24). Offline play and other platforms are not counted. Reviews newer than 30 days are left out of walk-away rates.
  • Editing a review may refresh its "playtime at review" figure. Without edited reviews the walk-away rate of negative reviewers is 34% (with them: 32%).
  • Reason labels come from a language model reading up to 1,200 characters per review in its original language. In a second-model check of 170 random labels across languages, 92% of main reasons were judged correct and 93% of quit-statement tags; expect roughly that error rate in reason shares.
  • "Walked away" is set at under 1 more hour. At under 2 hours the negative-reviewer rate is 37% and 9 of 9 complaint groups keep the same verdict; at under 5 hours it is 46% and 9 of 9 keep it (rank correlation with the 1-hour ordering: 0.97 and 0.95).
  • Groups that complain about different things also differ in hours played, purchase timing and expectations; the regression in section 1 controls for some of this, not all.

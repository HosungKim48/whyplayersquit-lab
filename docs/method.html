Method & limits

How the numbers were made

Everything is computed by code in the public repository from public Steam data. This page lists each step, each definition, and what the results cannot tell you.

Data

  • Source: Steam's documented public review endpoint (IUserReviewsService/GetAppReviews), reviews in all languages, full history, walked one calendar month at a time. Official announcements and patch notes come from Steam's public news endpoint (ISteamNews/GetNewsForApp).
  • Politeness: one request at a time per game with a pause between requests (about one request per second overall), back-off on errors, every raw page cached locally so nothing is fetched twice.
  • Privacy: raw review text and reviewer IDs never leave the local cache. The repository and this site contain only aggregated counts and short English paraphrases written by a model and screened by code (20 words or fewer; no 4-word run shared with the source review).
  • Snapshot: English reviews were fetched on 2026-11-24 and other languages on 2026-11-24 (UTC), up to a day apart. Playtime totals are as of each review's fetch.

Coverage gap: Steam's lifetime summary count is 2.2% higher than the reviews the month-by-month listing returned (1.0%–3.8% by game). The listing itself was fetched almost completely. The cheap filter runs in code first: positive reviews are not sent to the model, nor are negative reviews under 3 words (or under 4 characters in Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Thai, which are written without spaces). Reviews the model failed to return a valid line for stay unlabeled.

Definitions

Show all definitions (walk-away, intervals, adjusted rates, regression, markets, spikes)
  • Walked away: the reviewer's public total playtime at the snapshot is less than 1 hour above their playtime when they wrote the review. Computed only for reviews at least 30 days old.
  • Played 10+ more hours: same gap, 10 hours or more.
  • Played in last 2 weeks: Steam's two-week playtime figure is above zero at the snapshot.
  • Refunded: Steam's own flag on the review.
  • 95% interval: Wilson interval for a share. Rates on fewer than 30 reviews are not shown.
  • Adjusted for hours played: the walk-away rate a complaint group would have if its reviewers had the same spread of hours-at-review as all labeled negative reviewers of that game (direct standardization over five playtime bands). Not shown when a band is too thin.
  • Leave more often / stay more often / about average: a complaint group is called higher (or lower) only when its whole 95% interval sits above (or below) the game's overall negative-reviewer rate.
  • Market: the language the review is written in, as tagged by Steam. A language gets its own row in a game's report at 300+ reviews.
  • Likely owner: a routing suggestion that maps each complaint type to the kind of team that usually handles it. It is not derived from data.
  • Spike week: after the first six weeks, a week with at least 40 negative reviews, at least double the median of the previous eight weeks, and not below any of them.
  • Regression: logistic regression of walked-away on main complaint (reference: crashes & bugs), hours-at-review band, review language (languages under 300 reviews grouped), review timing (first 30 days, days 31–180, later), edited flag, and game in the pooled model. Complaint groups under 100 reviews are merged. Reported as odds ratios with 95% intervals.
  • Sensitivity: every verdict is recomputed with "walked away" set at under 2 and under 5 further hours; each report's limits section gives the result.
  • Sub-reasons: for Civilization VII only, reviews whose main complaint is gameplay & design were sorted in a second pass into ten design choices.

Reason labels

A small language model (Claude Haiku 5.5) read each negative review (first 1,200 characters, in its original language) and returned one main reason, an optional second reason, and whether the reviewer wrote that they had quit or would wait for fixes. Review text was treated as untrusted data.

Accuracy check

Blue = work by Claude Haiku 5.5. Orange = a check by something other than the author: Claude Fable 5.2 on the labels, code on the paraphrases and the published numbers.

Samples are drawn by code with fixed seeds: 10 per game in English, 10 per game in other languages, plus 50 across everything. For long reviews in the last two samples the checker read the first 420 characters. Overall, main reason judged correct: 92% (157 of 170); quit-statement tag: 93% (158 of 170).

Limits

  • Reviewers are not players. Only a small share of buyers write a review, and they are not a random sample. Nothing here estimates churn for the whole player base.
  • Correlation, not cause. A complaint group with a high walk-away rate was not shown to quit because of that complaint. Groups also differ in hours played, purchase timing and expectations.
  • Language is not country. Many players review in English wherever they live; regional pricing, launch timing and hardware differ by market in ways reviews do not record.
  • Review habits differ by community. Some language communities write shorter or more negative reviews in general, so compare a language with itself across games before comparing languages.
  • Playtime is imperfect. Offline play, other platforms and idle time are not separated. Editing a review may refresh its "playtime at review" value; each report shows the walk-away rate with and without edited reviews.
  • Stopping is not a lost customer. These are paid games. A reviewer who stops after 80 hours may simply have finished, while one who stops after 2 never got started, so read each walk-away rate next to hours played at review. Recommended reviewers at the same hours are the baseline for how often players stop without complaining.
  • The first two hours overlap Steam's refund window. Refunds are available up to 2 hours of play, so some under-2-hour reviewers stopped to stay eligible, whatever they complained about.
  • A review shows when a player wrote, not when they decided. A review is often written at the moment a player stops, more so a negative one. Total playtime is as of the snapshot, so players who are still playing will end up with their review earlier in their playtime than shown.
  • Six games, chosen by hand. All drew heavy criticism; patterns may not hold for well-received or free-to-play games.
  • Model labels contain errors at about the rate shown in the accuracy check, possibly more in smaller languages, and one main reason per review hides multi-issue reviews.
  • Patch matching is by date only. Official posts near a spike are listed for context; sales, free weekends and outside news also move review counts.

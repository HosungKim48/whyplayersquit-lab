Case study · Monster Hunter Wilds

The review score that hid a market

Read in English, this game has a complaint problem. Read in every language, it has a market problem. Here is how I got from one to the other, and what I would do with it.

Analysis run with an AI agent · numbers from the report data · full report

The question

The first version of this project read English reviews only. On that view the game looked healthy enough: 29% of 84,926 English reviews were negative, and the dominant complaint was performance. The obvious advice was "keep patching performance".

Then I pulled every language. English turned out to be only 42% of the reviews. So the question became simple: does the other 58% say the same thing?

Three things stand out to me.

  • The gap is large. Chinese (Simplified) reviews are 78% negative against 29% in English. That is not a rounding difference between communities; it is a different verdict.
  • The unhappy players are not people who bounced. The median Japanese critic had played 124.4 hours when they wrote the review (English critics: 34.7). These are invested players.
  • Most of them kept playing. Across all languages only 24% of critics logged under one more hour after reviewing. The anger and the engagement sit in the same people.

When it happened

The launch month (2025-03) brought 95,152 reviews at 37% negative. The worst stretch came later: 2025-06 ran at 82% negative on 15,479 reviews. The weeks with the sharpest jumps:

  • Week of 2025-06-16: 3,590 negative reviews against a usual 616. Nearby official posts: Update Ver. 1.011 — Maintenance Notice; Street Fighter 6 Special Collaboration - Coming May 28, 2025.
  • Week of 2025-06-30: 5,847 negative reviews against a usual 891. Nearby official posts: A digital event bringing you the latest news from Capcom!; Free Title Update 2 - Available June 30, 2025.
  • Week of 2025-09-29: 2,486 negative reviews against a usual 401. Nearby official posts: Guild Reports - Community Information Hub & Recent Posts!; The Showcase for the Free Title Update 3 (Ver. 1.030) is here!.

Review spikes sit next to official update posts. That is a lead, not a finding: sales and outside news move review counts too.

What I would tell the studio

PublishingStop reading the English score as the score

The store shows each visitor reviews in their own language. A team that tracks the English number is tracking the experience of 42% of reviewers.

Live ops & communityTreat this as a win-back situation, not a lost audience

60% of critics played 10 or more further hours, and 6% were active in the two weeks before the snapshot. They are reachable, and they are telling you what they want fixed.

EngineeringCheck performance by region before assuming one cause

The complaint mix differs by language. Hardware, storefront pricing and typical session length differ by region too, and reviews cannot separate them.

What I would need before believing it

  • Telemetry by region. Review language is a stand-in for market. Actual crash rates, frame times and session lengths by country would confirm or kill this.
  • Review habits by community. Some language communities review more harshly in general. The fair comparison is each language against itself on other games, which the markets table starts to show.
  • Human-checked labels in each language. The complaint labels were written by a small AI model and spot-checked by a larger one, not by native speakers.
  • Cause. Nothing here shows that a patch, a price or a performance problem made anyone leave.

What I took from it

The method did not change between the two versions of this project. The sample did. The English-only version was internally consistent, carefully caveated, and pointed at the wrong priority. Since then I check who is missing from the data before I look at what the data says.
